Branch diagnostic verification and interpretation
2026-11-23

Scope and evidence

This is a bounded correctness/interpretation check of results/diagnostic-001.json,
not a runtime experiment or a replacement for the timing campaign. No solver,
numerical-core, result, or benchmark source was changed. Two small in-memory
checks used one BLAS thread and the isolated cs6220-project-venv environment;
the successful checks together took less than two seconds of command wall time.

The recorded diagnostic contains 20 synthetic solves: smooth/random targets,
five seeds, lambda in {1e-3, 1e-5}, 640 total examples, 512 training examples,
k=16, checkpoints every 20 iterations, four sequential candidates, and at most
three accepted checkpoints per solve. Branches start from the same iterate and
both restart PCG with their respective fixed preconditioner. Diagnostic timing
and dense references are excluded from production timing.

Reading the actual results confirms:

Checkpoints within one solve are dependent; the 60 checkpoints are not 60
independent experimental replicates.

Independent checks

1. An independently assembled dense Gaussian kernel and direct Cholesky solve
   reproduced the saved reference solution exactly in this check.
2. Dense preconditioners formed from the represented basis/eigenvalues and
   independently factored by Cholesky reproduced M^{-1}r with relative errors
   5.92e-12 (old) and 5.99e-12 (new). This checks the inverse actually used by
   the solver rather than a differently truncated pseudoinverse.
3. A separate dense PCG calculation, with preconditioners constructed directly
   as C solve(W,C^T)+lambda I, reproduced the short-horizon ordering: at 5 steps
   old/new energies were 384.9453/440.5709, and at 10 steps 192.0665/267.5781.
   At 20 steps they were 72.2427/49.1784, retaining this checkpoint's new-better
   ordering but differing from the exact replay values. Algebraically equivalent
   inverse/residual arithmetic changes finite-precision Krylov trajectories.
   Thus this independent calculation verifies the qualitative mismatch, not
   bitwise equality of long branch trajectories. The recorded source version
   remains the definition of the reported measurements.
4. The first restarted-PCG step has direction z=M^{-1}r and step length
   alpha=(r^T z)/(z^T A z). Its exact squared-energy decrease is

       Delta(M;r) = (r^T z)^2 / (z^T A z).

   At this checkpoint, Delta_old=76.4474464033972 and
   Delta_new=73.24543360706842. Directly measuring the energy changes after
   one implemented step gives 76.44744640323358 and 73.24543360715359.
   The decrease formula and measured changes agree to about 2e-10 absolute.

Interpretation

No branch-sign, restart-asymmetry, energy-definition, or inverse-application bug
was found in the representative check. The new preconditioner tightens q while
giving a worse first PCG step and worse 5-/10-step errors. This is direct evidence
against using smaller q alone as a reliable short-horizon convergence criterion
on these development cases. The existing 20-step results are mixed and the total
time objective remains to be measured by the timing campaign.

The inverse-ordering inequality is intact: q bounds the current squared energy
error. Changing the preconditioner changes that bound, but does not change the
current iterate or its true error. Subsequent improvement depends on the direction
M^{-1}r and its A-curvature. Delta contains both the numerator q^2 and denominator
z^T A z, whereas minimizing q alone does not control their ratio. Delta is also
invariant under a positive scalar rescaling of the inverse preconditioner.

The current diagnostic already controls restart loss when comparing the two
preconditioners: both branches restart from the same x. Restart loss therefore
cannot explain their difference. It can still contribute separately to an
adaptive solver's total cost relative to uninterrupted fixed-preconditioner PCG.

Smallest next discriminating diagnostic

Keep the same dataset, seeds, column budget, tolerances, and accepted checkpoint
locations. At each checkpoint, preserve the OLD PCG recurrence immediately
before applying the proposed update, including x, recursive r, p, rho, and the
fixed old preconditioner. Run three diagnostic branches for the existing
5-/10-/20-step horizons:

A. Continue the old recurrence without restarting or changing M.
B. Restart from x using the old M and the directly checked residual.
C. Restart from x using the proposed new M and the same checked residual.

B versus C measures the effect of replacing columns conditional on restart;
A versus B measures the cost of discarding the old recurrence. Record the true
residual and squared energy error on all branches. These branches remain outside
production timings. The current callback exposes x and preconditioners but not
p/rho, so continuation requires a diagnostic-only recurrence snapshot rather
than reconstructing or inventing the omitted state from x alone.

Also record Delta_old and Delta_new at those checkpoints. This requires one
additional A*z product per preconditioner being checked and no exact solution.
Its agreement with first-step energy reduction is an algebraic identity; its
ability to predict 5-/10-/20-step progress is a separate empirical question.

If a later controlled selection experiment is warranted, a bounded option is to
compare the existing q-selected candidate with the no-op using Delta before
acceptance, while charging both additional A*z products. This tests whether
requiring a larger first-step energy decrease repairs the observed mismatch. It is not a claim of a new
method, and a better first step would not establish better multi-step convergence
or lower total runtime. Scoring all k removals with additional full kernel
matvecs would have a different cost and should not be called an inexpensive
small-matrix exchange without measuring that work.
