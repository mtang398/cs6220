The brainstorming and creative-thinking preflights suggest changing the

1. AFN with fixed-nnz sparse inverse-pattern exchanges

Partition A using k landmark indices as

    A = [[A11,C^T],[C,A22]],  L L^T=A11,  B=C L^{-T},
    S = A22-B B^T.

S is SPD. Its individual entries require kernel pairs and inner products of
the cached rows of B, not new full columns. For a lower-triangular row pattern
J containing its diagonal i, canonical FSAI coefficients are

    u = S[J,J]^{-1} e_i,        g[J] = u/sqrt(u_i).

Each row has g^T S g=1 and a positive diagonal. Consequently H=G^T G is SPD
at any retained pattern. Applying the full AFN inverse to residual r uses

    t = L^{-1}r1,       q = r2-B t,
    z2 = H q,          z1 = L^{-T}(t-B^T z2).

This preserves k actual landmark columns but adds sparse G. Count B, L,
permutation, sparse values and indices; comparisons require fixed total bytes,
not just k. Static AFN is established, and its sparse precision correction
differs from adding block covariance to Nyström. The static diagnostic showed
substantial headroom on Concrete, weaker results on smooth data, and no clear
advantage on Airfoil; that finding is not a solve-adaptive contribution.

An exact score on remaining-residual right-hand sides

For recent Schur residuals q_l, define z_l=H q_l, d_l=q_l-S z_l, and

    J(H) = sum_l w_l [z_l^T S z_l - 2 q_l^T z_l].

The unknown constant sum_l w_l q_l^T S^{-1}q_l turns J into
sum_l w_l ||Hq_l-S^{-1}q_l||_S^2. Thus differences of J are exact differences
of inverse-action error in the Schur energy norm. This includes the curvature
term missing from a bare q^T Hq score. It is still not a multistep convergence
guarantee. With several residuals, fix positive weights before comparing
candidates, for example w_l=1/(q_l^T H_old q_l).

Replacing one canonical row g by h changes its action by

    delta_z_l = h(h^T q_l)-g(g^T q_l),
    delta_J = sum_l w_l [-2 d_l^T delta_z_l
                         + delta_z_l^T S delta_z_l].

Only the union of the old/new sparse patterns supports delta_z_l. Candidate
scores therefore require small actual Schur blocks. For multiple changed rows,
sum their action differences before evaluating the quadratic term: cross terms
must not be discarded. A fixed-nnz donor/recipient exchange has the same form.
Canonical coefficients always come from S, not a fit to solver secants.

Ordinary PCG provides the score data without another production kernel product
if implemented carefully. From p=z+beta p_old and the ordinary Ap, recover
Az=Ap-beta Ap_old. Then

    S z2 = (Az)2-B L^{-1}(Az)1.

The offline test instead explicitly charged one extra dense S product for the
initial score and another product per selected rule for combined verification.
One early update/restart, followed by a frozen preconditioner, separates useful
pattern selection from recurrent disruption. Strong controls are static AFN,
aFSAI/Kaporin pattern improvement, and an unchanged matched restart.

Replacing exposure by exact-error coordinate squares is not a justified next
oracle. With B=G S G^T, u=Gq, and e=S^{-1}q=G^T v, v=B^{-1}u contains the
coefficients of e in the nonorthogonal canonical row basis. The true energy is

overlap with e and on other rows. Thus weighting determinant gains by v_i^2

whether accurate error information can support a useful allocation mechanism.

2. Hierarchical conditional couplings under a byte cap

A substantively different representation keeps accurate small diagonal blocks
and compresses interactions between spatial clusters. This changes the original
actual-column constraint to a fixed total factor-storage budget. Leaf factors,
off-diagonal bases, cores, indices and workspaces all count. A binary geometry
tree and one early redistribution/restart are enough for an initial study;
a complete HODLR production implementation should wait for a capacity test.

A two-block calculation identifies the correct inverse-sensitive quantity.
With exact diagonal factorizations A11=L1 L1^T and A22=L2 L2^T, whiten the
coupling T=L1^{-1} A12 L2^{-T}. Since A is SPD, all its singular values satisfy
sigma_j<1. Retaining any subset of T's SVD modes leaves
[[I,T_ret],[T_ret^T,I]] SPD. This preserves positive definiteness without
inserting unrelated covariance in the discarded complement.

For whitened residual q=(L1^{-1}r1,L2^{-1}r2), write a_j=u_j^T q1 and
b_j=v_j^T q2. Omitting coupling mode (u_j,sigma_j,v_j) gives the exact
two-block inverse-action error contribution

    sigma_j^2 [(a_j+b_j)^2/(2(1+sigma_j))
                 +(a_j-b_j)^2/(2(1-sigma_j))].

The difference component is particularly sensitive when sigma_j approaches
one. Thus compressing unwhitened blocks by singular magnitude alone can miss
the modes important for the inverse. Sum this expression over recent residuals
and compare its reduction per stored scalar when considering exchanges of
coupling modes between nodes. At a single exact merge this is an exact energy
calculation. Scores across approximate nested children are not additive without
further analysis; approximate children can also make a whitened coupling's
norm exceed one. Those are explicit factorization/stability problems to resolve,
not details covered by the two-block identity.

First informative experiment: on the existing small workloads, inspect a
geometric tree's whitened coupling spectra and compare a static storage-matched
hierarchical factor with global Nyström. Include equal-rank, approximation-error
allocation, and residual-weighted allocation at the same bytes. Dense local SVDs
may diagnose representational headroom, but their cost is not a production
claim. Only if a frozen hierarchical factor shows material headroom should an
entry-based construction and one adaptive reallocation be implemented. Retain
all datasets/regularizations in evaluation; do not restrict to favorable low-
dimensional geometry or bandwidths. The present sample sizes may be too small
to repay hierarchical setup, which the eventual total-time test must decide.

Hierarchical preconditioning is established; Chen and Martinsson (2022),
https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06290, gives HODLR factorizations with kernel-system
applications. Solver-dependent block refinement is also established: Bauer
and Bebendorf, Block-adaptive Cross Approximation of Discrete Integral
Operators (2019), https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01918, adapts block accuracy to
solution error while interlacing assembly and solution. Hard-memory coarsening
and inverse-sensitive stable factor allocation would therefore need distinct
evidence beyond naming residual-guided hierarchical ranks.

Closed-form pair model and a score against learned diagonal correction

First fit the diagonal control from the same secants:

    c_i = <P[i,:],Y[i,:]>,   g_i = ||P[i,:]||^2,
    d_i = clip(c_i/g_i, 0, e_i),
    gain_i = 2 d_i c_i - d_i^2 g_i.

A zero input row gets d_i=0. For a candidate pair B={i,j}, let v be the unit
top eigenvector of the 2-by-2 matrix Y_B Y_B^T. This fixes a locally dominant
observed residual direction without querying new kernel entries. Define

    t = v^T P_B,       c = <t,v^T Y_B>,       g = ||t||^2,
    a_max = min_{ell in B: v_ell^2>0} e_ell/v_ell^2,
    a = clip(c/g, 0, a_max),       D_B = a v v^T,
    gain_B = 2 a c - a^2 g.

Skip negligible Y_B or g. The formula for a exactly minimizes
||Y_B-a v v^T P_B||_F^2 over this one-parameter PSD family. The cap ensures
diag(D_B)<=diag(E_BB); it does not imply D<=E. Negative off-diagonal entries
are permitted when the two components of v have opposite signs.

Score a pair by gain_B-gain_i-gain_j. Sort positive scores and greedily take
disjoint pairs; leave unmatched entries at their fitted d_i. Thus the training
objective cannot worsen relative to the learned diagonal control. The vector
v was chosen from Y_B, so this is exact only within the stated rank-one family,
not a claim to solve the full PSD block-fitting problem.

Avoid an O(n^2) search in the actual solver. A concrete first candidate graph
queries a fixed small number of nearest neighbors of both signs of each
normalized row Y[i,:] in h dimensions. Include pairs with nearly opposite
signatures because PSD blocks can have negative off-diagonal entries. Ignore
negligible rows, deduplicate the graph, and cap its degree before scoring.
Use the same graph size for random-pair and input-neighbor controls. Candidate
search time counts; an exhaustive small-n diagnostic is not the production cost.

SPD and a stable cheap inverse

Every block is PSD, so B=lambda I+D is SPD and M_new=B+F F^T is SPD. Unlike
unmodified Nyström, M_new<=A is not guaranteed, so its old error upper bound
and SR1 secant-preservation arguments do not apply. A training fit is not an
exact global secant condition, nor does it preserve past CG conjugacy.

Apply T=B^{-1/2} blockwise. On a pair with perpendicular unit vectors v,w,

    T_B r_B = v(v^T r_B)/sqrt(lambda+a)
              + w(w^T r_B)/sqrt(lambda).

This avoids subtracting two large nearly equal terms. Form T F=U Sigma V^T
once, then apply

    M_new^{-1}r = T (I+U Sigma^2 U^T)^{-1} T r.

Use the established twice-projected span/complement inverse, dividing the span
coefficients by 1+sigma_i^2. Setup costs O(nk^2+k^3); applications cost O(nk+n).
Final extra block storage is O(n), in addition to the original k columns and
the whitened inverse basis. Do not retain an unnecessary duplicate original
basis after freezing. Construction has 2nh secant storage and coexisting factors;
both temporary bytes and peak RSS count. A fixed k-column budget is preserved;
total rank is no longer k, and total memory is slightly larger.

An independent n=32,k=5,h=4,lambda=1e-3 algebra check found maximum gain-identity
error 3.55e-15, no residual-diagonal cap violations, minimum preconditioner
eigenvalue lambda to roundoff, and inverse relative error 1.14e-12 against a
dense solve. These check the fit and inverse, not a benchmark outcome.

Generalized Nyström and its exact SR1 interpretation

For a conceptual probe matrix Omega, retain Y=K Omega and W=Omega^T K Omega.
The approximation and preconditioner are

    Khat = Y W^dagger Y^T,       M = lambda I + Khat.

In exact arithmetic Khat Omega=K Omega, so M Omega=A Omega. Full-rank formulas
below use W^{-1}; singular or numerically truncated cores require the actual
represented approximation. Inserting p gives u=(K-Khat)p=Ap-Mp and

    delta = p^T u,
    Khat_aug = Khat + u u^T/delta,
    M_aug = M + u u^T/delta.

This is the standard symmetric rank-one (SR1) Hessian update with secant pair
(p,Ap). It is positive when delta>0 because K-Khat is positive semidefinite.
If delta is numerically negligible, skip insertion rather than divide by it.
The SR1 identity is established algebra, not a new update formula.

Dense Omega need not be stored. New cross entries are Y^T p and p^T Kp, while
a new coordinate j has cross entries Y[j,:]. Store Y, W, and the Gram G=Y^T Y;
the inverse application's basis/eigenvalues add their usual n-by-k storage.
Normalize incoming p and Kp by sqrt(p^T Kp) when that quantity is reliable.
Coordinate and learned probes can then share a unit-diagonal kernel core.
General probes require an explicit core: columns[indices,:] is no longer valid.

All-removal scores, including rejection of the new probe

For the augmented k+1 probes, define V=W^{-1} and H=W+G/lambda. Deleting probe i
has the exact scores

    trace(Khat_{-i}) = trace(VG) - (V G V)_ii / V_ii,

    logdet(M_{-i}) - n log(lambda)
      = logdet(H) - logdet(W) + log((H^{-1})_ii) - log(V_ii).

Thus one small factorization per augmented matrix scores all removals. Maximize
captured trace or regularized log determinant, and include deletion of p as the
unchanged state. They measure global approximation quality; neither guarantees
remaining-error reduction. A fixed minimum gain or numerical tie tolerance
must be chosen before evaluation, not adjusted using favorable solves.

Rebuild and check the winner with the stable applied representation. If its
positive eigenvalues are theta, the actual scores are sum(theta) and
sum(log1p(theta/lambda)). Near-singular cores require stable direct evaluation;
the inverse-diagonal identity must not be mixed with truncated factors.
Incoming Y^T p, Y^T Kp, and ||Kp||^2 cost O(nk); all small scores cost O(k^3).
Accepted stable QR/SVD rebuilds can cost O(nk^2), which may dominate frequent
updates. Temporary old/augmented/new factors, the n-by-k basis, and peak RSS
all count. Reuse Ap; no new full operator product is intrinsic to this update.

Compatibility with the solve and the effect of eviction

If a retained learned direction d satisfies Md=Ad and d^T r=0, then

    d^T A M^{-1}r = d^T r = 0.

After an exact line search along the current p, the new residual is orthogonal
to p. If p was A-orthogonal to other retained learned directions, their residual
orthogonality is preserved too. An accepted update that retains p can therefore
restart with M_new^{-1}r while preserving conjugacy to the retained learned
directions. An eviction removes the guarantee for the discarded direction.
Coordinate probes have no corresponding residual-orthogonality guarantee.
This is limited retained conjugacy, never a full-history CG guarantee.

Do not carry ordinary fixed-PCG beta through an accepted preconditioner change.
Rejecting the new probe leaves the preconditioner and ordinary recurrence intact.
Accepted updates restart the recurrence; the retained secants supply only the
orthogonality just stated. Finite precision, true-residual replacement, and
core truncation can spoil those identities. Before discarding the current p,
check ||M_new^{-1}Ap-p||/||p|| for an accepted insertion. Without storing old
probe vectors, all old residual orthogonalities cannot be explicitly audited;
extra direction storage for such checks must be counted.

An important limiting case prevents overinterpreting the mechanism. If every
direction is retained from iteration zero, exact SR1 updates plus exact line
searches produce directions collinear with fixed-initial-preconditioner CG.
For p=M^{-1}r, write sigma=p^T Mp, eta=p^T Ap, tau=(Ap)^T M^{-1}(Ap). After
the line search and SR1 update, the new inverse-residual direction is

    [(eta-sigma)/(eta (tau-eta))] [tau p-eta M^{-1}Ap],

whereas the ordinary PCG next direction is

    (sigma/eta^2) [tau p-eta M^{-1}Ap].

The nonzero positive factors are immaterial to the next exact line search.
Degenerate cases need a skip/breakdown treatment. Learning alone therefore
does not establish a stronger exact-arithmetic solver. The scientific question
is whether allocating the fixed representation between global coordinate
information and observed directions improves time or numerical robustness.

Required discriminating controls

Compare fixed rank-k PCG with both RPCholesky and deterministic largest-diagonal
initialization; retain the diagonal-corrected control described below. Compare
pure learned limited-memory SR1, FIFO replacement, a fixed allocation of k0
coordinates plus k-k0 learned probes, and score-selected allocation. A growing
no-eviction diagnostic checks the CG-equivalent limiting case but is not a
fixed-budget competitor. Memory-matched L-BFGS and fixed Nyström plus explicit
limited-history orthogonalization are important strong controls. Match actual
stored n-vectors: Y plus its inverse-application basis already costs about 2nk,
so k BFGS secant pairs can be a fairer comparison than only floor(k/2) pairs.
Do not retain rank k columns plus extra history and call that the same budget.
Use the full original dataset, rank, regularization, and paired-seed scope;
count rejected work, rebuilds, true residual checks, and all operator products.

Diagnostic option superseded by the failed full-space oracle

Full-space lookahead can compare branch corrections s from the same x0,r0 using

    squared-energy decrease = 2 r0^T s - s^T A s.

Both s and As accumulate from branch steps, so selection needs no exact solution
or extra scoring product. The unchanged branch must continue its real recurrence;
replacement branches must use exactly the intended restart/history policy.
Batching branch directions shares kernel construction but all right-hand-side
products and storage count. If a branch wins, commit its complete solver state
without another restart. Root reported an offline oracle with 32 candidates,
all removals, k=16/32, and checkpoints 5/20: none beat old continuation after
5 or 10 steps, and even the best new gain was often about half the old gain.
This supplied development result supersedes further lookahead-shortlist tuning.

The same screens found frozen final column sets close to the initial set:
initial 137/138 iterations; curvature-final 141 versus adaptive 322;
energy-final 137 versus adaptive 262; direction-final 140 versus adaptive 273.
Improved spectral condition numbers did not translate to those target solves.
The Schur-complement coarse-space screen subsequently found new sampled columns
worse in all eight inspected cases. These motivate changing the retained probe
family, not claiming success for a better scalar approximation score.

The earlier projected-Delta proposal below is superseded as the preferred route.
revision-screen-001 records 138 iterations for fixed FCG with history 8, versus

The then-proposed distinct criterion fits curvature on several solver directions,
not optimize the correction from the current residual alone. Root has implemented

inverse-trace alternative, which puts a stronger penalty on poorly represented

Subspace and objective

Take a small independent set D of recent correction/secant directions and the
current preconditioned residual. Retain their known A-images AD. Prior directions
and their images already exist in the limited-history solver. The current
direction's A-image can be the operator product needed for the next ordinary
step and must be reused; otherwise count one extra product. Do not use only old
directions: in exact CG, the current residual is orthogonal to their span.

Remove dependencies and form U with U^T A U=I_s, for example by factoring
D^T A D=L L^T and taking U=D L^{-T}, AU=AD L^{-T}. Use a rank-revealing fallback
when this Gram is unreliable. Merely normalizing each column's A-norm does not
make the columns mutually A-orthogonal. This distinction matters for the score.
Keep U fixed during that checkpoint's candidate/removal comparisons.

For the original M_S=Khat_S+lambda I, define

    B_S = U^T M_S U,
    J(S) = trace(B_S^{-1}).

Because 0 < M_S <= A, the eigenvalues mu_a of B_S lie in (0,1], and
J(S)=sum_a 1/mu_a >= s. These reciprocals are the generalized A/M Rayleigh--Ritz
eigenvalues restricted to span(U). Minimizing J improves curvature coverage of
several observed directions and penalizes small mu much more strongly than
maximizing sum log(mu). It is not the trace of U^T M_S^{-1} U: inversion and
projection do not commute. Nor is it a bound on the full system's condition
number or on the current solution error.

If an unwhitened basis D is used instead, the basis-invariant equivalent is

    J(S) = trace[(D^T M_S D)^{-1}(D^T A D)].

The causal hypothesis is that a preconditioner which fits the curvature of
several directions already needed by the solve will avoid repeatedly making
those directions expensive after history truncation. Including the current
direction allows the objective to follow newly exposed modes. This is a
multi-direction spectral-fit hypothesis, not a guaranteed convergence result.

Exact all-removal score without full operator products

For augmented T=S union {j}, let C=K[:,T], W=K[T,T], V=W^{-1}, F=C^T U,
Y=V F, and B=lambda U^T U+F^T V F. For deletion i,

    a_i = Y[i,:]^T/sqrt(V_ii),
    B_i = B - a_i a_i^T,
    J_i = trace(B^{-1})
          + ||B^{-1} a_i||_2^2/(1-a_i^T B^{-1} a_i).

Use small solves; do not form any n-by-n inverse. A small denominator requires
a direct Cholesky/eigendecomposition of B_i rather than a fragile downdate.
The unchanged set is deletion of j. These identities assume the untruncated
positive definite selected core. For truncated states, evaluate U^T M_S U from
the actual stored basis/eigenvalues, rebuild stable trial states as needed, and
verify the winning score against the state that would be applied.

A global-quality guard can prevent improving the inspected subspace by severely
damaging the rest of K. The residual trace is available from the existing Gram
G=C^T C. For deletion i,

    trace(K-Khat_{T\{i}})
      = n - trace(VG) + (V[:,i]^T G V[:,i])/V_ii.

For a first controlled variant, require this trace to remain no greater than
the initialization's trace apart from numerical tolerance, and require a stable
decrease of J. Record how often this guard blocks the best local-curvature
replacement. A separate guard-off ablation distinguishes a poor score from an
overrestrictive safeguard. Do not silently relax the guard only on favorable
cases. Trace protection controls global average approximation, not every
unobserved direction.

Candidate rule matched to the inverse-trace objective

Let E=K-Khat_S, d_j=E_jj, and e_j=U^T E[:,j]. For a full-rank Nyström state,
adding j changes B_S to B_S+e_j e_j^T/d_j. The exact reduction in J from this
addition is

    gain_j = ||B_S^{-1} e_j||_2^2
             / (d_j + e_j^T B_S^{-1} e_j).

Rank or sample these gains over unselected indices with reliably positive d_j.
All e_j are rows of EU=AU-lambda U-Khat_S U. Hence the rule uses already
available secant/operator information and O(nks+ns^2) local work, without an
additional full operator application per candidate. It concentrates acquisition
on weak generalized modes because of B_S^{-1}. Uniform candidates and the
unweighted sum((EU)[j,:]^2)/d_j rule provide distinct generator controls.
Actual fixed-budget deletion still uses J_i; an attractive addition is not
automatically a useful exchange.

A fresh n=40 Gaussian algebra check with k=6, s=4, lambda=0.01 compared all
removal scores against directly constructed preconditioners. Maximum relative
score discrepancy was 3.56e-15. The addition-gain identity agreed to 5.70e-16
relative; ||U^T A U-I|| was 6.57e-16. These are algebra checks only.

Costs and the smallest useful comparison

After U,AU are available, each candidate needs one kernel column, O(n(k+s))
inner products, O(k^3) augmented-core work, and O(ks^2+s^3) inverse-trace scores.
Initial construction of F and EU costs O(nks); their small matrices are O(k^2+s^2).
Accepted state rebuilds retain their existing O(nk^2+k^3) cost. Keeping U and AU
explicitly costs 2ns entries, although an existing history and small coordinate
map can avoid duplicating those arrays. Count any current-direction operator
product not reused by the solve. This avoids the earlier Delta scheme's
k+1 full right-hand-side products per candidate. k kernel columns remain fixed.

First compare the implemented logdet criterion with inverse trace using the same
U, candidate indices, recurrence/history, and trace-guard policy; then compare
their matched candidate generators separately. Inspect the projected generalized
eigenvalues and 5/10/20-step branch progress with the same continuation state.
An initial single-update-and-freeze comparison distinguishes a better retained
state from frequent adaptation that damages Krylov progress. Repeated adaptation
must subsequently earn its cost. Keep all original datasets, rank budgets,
regularizations, seeds, accuracy checks, and total-time accounting in the broader
study; a development screen is not a replacement for that scope.

Strong static controls before crediting adaptation

1. Deterministic pivoted Cholesky: select the largest remaining residual diagonal
   at each acquisition, with the same k columns and the same stable inverse
   implementation. This is a standard greedy pivoting control, not a guaranteed
   improvement over RPCholesky. It tests whether the random starting set is weak.
2. Diagonal-corrected Nyström: use

       M_dc = Khat_S + diag(K-Khat_S) + lambda I.

   Its diagonal term is positive and it remains SPD; it needs one additional
   n-vector and O(nk) work to form the residual diagonal. With D=diag(K-Khat)+lambda I,
   factor D^{-1/2} F for Khat=F F^T and apply the low-rank inverse in those scaled
   coordinates. Setup is O(nk^2), application O(nk); count the actual basis and
   workspace. The low-rank-plus-diagonal covariance form is established in FITC
   Gaussian-process approximation, so this is not a new preconditioner claim.
   M_dc need not satisfy M_dc <= A. The earlier q upper bound and J>=s argument
   must not be transferred to this variant. If it wins statically, adaptation
   must be tested against that stronger baseline rather than only uncorrected RP.
3. Compare actual storage and total time with the already tested higher-rank
   fixed preconditioner. k=16 plus direction/history storage is not the same
   memory budget as a bare k=16 method. Report both retained-column budget and
   total memory; do not present a memory advantage if static k=24 is cheaper
   or equally sized and faster.

The standard concepts are Rayleigh--Ritz restriction, secant curvature fitting,
spectral equivalence, low-rank deletion/downdate identities, and adaptive
preconditioning. They motivate a mechanism, not its novelty. Chen et al.'s
RPCholesky paper is the primary pivoting reference:
https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.22234 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06503.
For low-rank-plus-diagonal covariance correction, Quiñonero-Candela and Rasmussen,
A Unifying View of Sparse Approximate Gaussian Process Regression, JMLR 6,
1939--1959 (2005), Section 6 and equations (21)--(22):
https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume6/quinonero-candela05a/quinonero-candela05a.pdf.
These primary sources were checked for the stated conceptual relationships.
Neither source establishes this criterion's usefulness. The negative screens
remain recorded evidence; a useful result still requires actual total-time
benefit at an honestly stated fixed storage budget.

Earlier proposal: projected single-step decrease

The earlier mechanism ranks fixed-budget column replacements by
the exact energy decrease of the direction the solver will actually take,
after projecting against its retained search directions. Include the unchanged
preconditioner in this ranking and keep the search history when a replacement
is accepted. This combines a measurable selection objective with a recurrence
that does not automatically discard the work represented by that history.

This document develops the criterion and its discriminating comparison. It is
not evidence of a working speed improvement or a novelty claim. The project
objective remains useful solve-adaptive preconditioning with k retained kernel
columns, across the original synthetic/real datasets and rank/regularization
settings. A negative result for the first score does not complete that objective.

Evidence motivating the change

results/initial-analysis.json contains 240 completed timed runs. Exchange/fixed
median paired time ratios were 2.24 on smooth synthetic targets, 2.36 on random
synthetic targets, 2.89 on Concrete, and 2.34 on Airfoil. All 20 paired cases were
slower with the original score. In diagnostic-001.json, all 60 inspected
checkpoints lowered q=r^T M^{-1}r, but every new-preconditioner branch had larger
squared energy error after both 5 and 10 steps. Both branches restarted, so their
difference cannot be attributed to unequal restart treatment.

The score q is a bound on the current error, not an objective for the next
correction. For example, multiplying an inverse preconditioner by a positive
scalar changes q but leaves an exact line-search correction unchanged. The
new criterion should therefore use the correction direction and its A-curvature.

1. Score the projected direction actually used

Let A=K+lambda I, lambda>0, r=y-Ax, and let D contain the retained solver
directions. Their A-images AD are available from previous iterations. For a
candidate retained set S_i with exactly k indices, form

    z_i = M_{S_i}^{-1} r,
    G_D = D^T A D,
    p_i = z_i - D G_D^{-1} D^T A z_i,
    A p_i = A z_i - (AD) G_D^{-1} D^T A z_i.

Use a stable small solve for G_D, or sequential A-orthogonalization with a
second pass. A diagonal normalization is valid only when the stored directions
are sufficiently A-orthogonal. When the history is empty, p_i=z_i.

For an exact line search from the current x,

    a_i = (r^T p_i)/(p_i^T A p_i),
    x_new = x + a_i p_i,
    Delta_i = (r^T p_i)^2/(p_i^T A p_i).

Delta_i is the exact decrease of squared energy error, e^T A e. To see this,
expand the error after a step: E_new=E-2a r^T p+a^2 p^T A p. Minimizing this
quadratic gives the expressions above. No exact solution is used in selection.
The score is invariant under nonzero scaling of p_i.

Compute r^T p_i explicitly. Replacing it by r^T z_i requires r^T D=0; true
residual replacement and finite precision can spoil that identity. Scoring and
the executed step must use the same residual and the same projected direction.

Rank every removal, including deletion of the new candidate itself. This no-op
must represent continuation with the current fixed-budget state and the same
history, not an artificially restarted old solver. Accept only a stable,
material improvement over that no-op. A small experimental gain margin such as
2% can be fixed before the first comparison; it is a practical screening
parameter, not a theorem that future iteration savings repay search cost.
Keep numerical acceptance tolerances distinct from this gain margin.

After acceptance, retain the history and use the selected verified p_i and A p_i
for the next step. A rejected checkpoint leaves the state and history unchanged.
The selected operator product should be reused; computing it again as the next
iteration's ordinary product would charge avoidable work to the method.

2. All removals can be generated from one augmented system

For one new column j, write T=S union {j}, C=K[:,T], W=K[T,T], b=C^T r, and

    H = W + C^T C/lambda,
    h = H^{-1} b,
    B = H^{-1}.

In exact arithmetic with W positive definite,

    z_aug = M_T^{-1} r = r/lambda - C h/lambda^2.

For deletion i, embed the smaller-system solution in a vector with zero at i.
The principal-deletion identity gives

    h_deleted = h - B[:,i] h_i/B_ii,
    z_i = z_aug + (C B[:,i]) h_i/(lambda^2 B_ii).

The plus sign and lambda^2 denominator are essential. Solve for B's columns
using the small Cholesky factors; this is a temporary small-system calculation,
not application of a dense n-by-n inverse. Form all k+1 directions in Z, then
compute AZ in one blockwise kernel matrix-matrix application. The kernel blocks
are constructed once for this candidate and multiplied by all right-hand sides.
History projection then uses Z and AZ without another operator application.

A small independent in-memory algebra check used n=40 Gaussian inputs, k+1=7,
lambda=1e-3, and cond(H)=1466.48. Every generated deletion direction matched a
directly rebuilt stable preconditioner application; the largest relative
difference was 1.00e-14. The one-step squared-energy identity agreed with direct
energy differences to 7.34e-10 absolute in that check. This checks the formula
in one well-conditioned example, not its reliability on every workload.

Numerical conditions are substantive here. A numerically truncated z_aug cannot
be combined blindly with the untruncated full-rank H deletion identity. Check
the augmented core and score-system conditioning. Verify the generated no-op
against the current state's stable apply(r). Rebuild the selected rank-k state
and check its z against the predicted direction before accepting it. Reproject
the rebuilt direction. If it differs materially, obtain its actual A-image and
recompute Delta, charging that extra product. A singular/unreliable case can
fall back to stable applications for each removal and still batch their A-images;
record the expensive fallback and use the same represented numerical rank as
the solver. The actual applied state, not the algebraically ideal formula,
defines the accepted update.

3. Cost and memory contract

For s=k+1 deletion directions, one batched call shares O(n^2 d) kernel-entry
construction for d input features, but its multiplication work is O(n^2 s).
It is not one scalar matrix-vector product. Record both the number of batched
operator calls and the number of right-hand-side products, together with their
wall time. For c sequential candidates, the straightforward implementation
uses c batches of s right-hand sides, before stable-verification fallbacks.

Z and AZ require 2n(k+1) floating-point entries. Retained history D and AD require
2nh entries for h history directions. The existing state retains its kernel
columns, orthonormal factor basis, and small matrices. A rebuilt candidate may
temporarily coexist with the old state. Kernel-block workspace is O(block_size*n).
Report these arrays and process peak RSS; k retained landmark indices does not
mean only nk floating-point entries are used. Give the fixed-FCG control exactly
the same history capacity and count its projection overhead too.

Batching is the preferable first implementation to retaining AC. An alternative
cache J=C^T A C can evaluate all denominators using one Ar and one A-times-new-
column product, but initializing it costs k products; its cancellation-prone
quadratic expression also needs care. Retaining AC uses another nk entries.
Those alternatives can be tested later if batched direction scoring dominates
time; they are not prerequisites for this candidate.

At k=16, candidate counts 1 and 4 are both already within the original planned
study. Compare them with checkpoint interval 20. Search may still be too costly
even when Delta improves. Measure whether saved ordinary iterations outweigh
the batched products, stable rebuilds, and history projections. Rejected searches
still consume time. No matrix-matrix speedup should be assumed from FLOP counts.

4. Candidate generation from the current kernel approximation residual

Keep the existing r_j^2 sampling in the first criterion comparison so that the
acceptance rule is isolated. If candidate quality limits useful exchanges, the
strongest separate generator to try uses the current continuation direction p
and its available Ap:

    E = K - Khat_S,
    E p = (Ap-lambda p) - Khat_S p,
    d_j = E_jj.

For a full-rank Nyström state, adding an unselected column j gives

    Khat_{S union {j}} - Khat_S = (E[:,j] E[j,:])/d_j,

so the captured kernel energy along p is

    p^T (Khat_{S union {j}}-Khat_S) p = (E p)_j^2/d_j.

This suggests sampling or shortlisting (E p)_j^2/d_j over unselected indices
with reliably positive d_j. It uses solver information and residual matrix
curvature rather than large equation residuals alone. Khat_S p and its diagonal
are available from the stored basis/eigenvalues in O(nk) work. With Ap reused,
there is no additional full operator product; workspace is a few n-vectors and
an optional n-entry diagonal cache. Guard small/negative numerical d_j values.
For truncated states the full-rank augmentation identity need not describe the
actual rebuilt factor, so this remains a generator; projected Delta on the
actual replacement state decides acceptance.

This generator is not a substitute for the fixed-budget removal criterion.
Growing a matrix approximation and replacing one of k retained columns are
different operations. Test this generator only after the score/recurrence
comparison, with the candidate count held fixed.

5. Discriminating comparison and interpretation

The decisive mechanism comparison is q selection versus projected-Delta selection
under the same limited-history flexible recurrence, initial columns, candidate
draws, true-residual policy, and history capacity. Use the same candidate stream
until differing accepted states make exact candidate identity impossible; keep
the random seeds paired and record candidate indices. A fixed-preconditioner
flexible solver with the same history distinguishes any benefit of the recurrence
from benefit of exchanging columns. Uninterrupted fixed PCG remains the main
runtime reference. Existing restarted runs remain evidence about the discarded
history cost; a projected-Delta/restart ablation can isolate that interaction.

At inspected checkpoints, branch old/new states with the same retained history
and compare actual squared-energy decrease after 1, 5, 10, and 20 steps. A dense
reference may measure these errors offline but must not enter selection. The
one-step identity is a correctness check; longer-horizon improvement is a
scientific test. Count all accepted, rejected, and unstable checkpoints, not
only successful branches. Group observations by solve rather than pretending
checkpoints are independent samples.

End-to-end evidence must include total time to the directly checked residual
tolerance, ordinary and batched operator work, selection/rebuild/projection time,
solution and prediction differences, accepted exchanges, and peak storage.
Compare candidate counts 1 and 4 before increasing search width. Continue the
original datasets (both synthetic target families, Concrete, Airfoil), five
paired seeds, rank budgets {16,32,64}, regularization {1e-1,1e-3,1e-5}, and the
planned frequency/target controls as the research proceeds. A bounded prototype
stage chooses an implementation; it does not reduce that scope or establish
the project's final contribution.

If projected Delta improves the first step but not 5--20-step progress, the
criterion is still too greedy; a useful next revision is to protect or score
several recent correction directions, not to claim the first-step identity
establishes convergence. If branch progress improves but time does not, reduce
candidate/search frequency or optimize the measured batching bottleneck. If
fixed FCG accounts for all improvement, column adaptation has not yet earned
its extra work. Useful total runtime at fixed k, rather than setup completion
or a more favorable bound, is the success condition.

6. Closest-work boundaries

Flexible CG with explicit direction orthogonalization is established. Notay,
Flexible Conjugate Gradients, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 22(4), 1444--1460 (2000),
DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827599362314, analyzes variable preconditioning
and truncated direction histories. The primary publisher abstract was checked.
This supports the recurrence choice; it does not prove a speedup for this
landmark replacement rule.

Knyazev and Lashuk, Steepest Descent and Conjugate Gradient Methods with Variable
Preconditioning, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 29(4), 1267--1280, published online
2007, DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/060675290; preprint
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605767. The primary abstract states that SPD variable
preconditioning with bounded condition numbers can still lose CG's improvement
